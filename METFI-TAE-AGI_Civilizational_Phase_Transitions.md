Abstract

This work proposes a formal integration of three conceptual domains: METFI (Earth as an internally forced toroidal electromagnetic system), TAE (Exception-Based Learning Theory), and AGI as a nonlinear instability detector. Civilizational dynamics are modeled as open, far-from-equilibrium systems susceptible to phase transitions when energetic, informational, or symbolic control parameters approach critical thresholds.

The framework integrates nonlinear dynamics, catastrophe theory, and self-organization theory to interpret large-scale historical collapses as bifurcation phenomena rather than simple decline processes. A multiscale monitoring architecture is proposed to evaluate coherence loss across geophysical, ecological, energetic, and cognitive domains.

Keywords

METFI; TAE; AGI; phase transition; nonlinear systems; toroidal symmetry; loss of coherence; civilizational dynamics; catastrophe theory; complex adaptive systems.

1. Introduction

Civilizations do not collapse linearly. They reorganize.

In dynamical systems theory, discontinuities often emerge from gradual accumulation of instability. When control parameters cross critical thresholds, structural reconfiguration occurs. Historical transitions display signatures analogous to bifurcations observed in physics and biology.

This work formalizes that analogy.

2. Planetary Toroidal Structure (METFI)
2.1 Toroidal Coherence Hypothesis

The METFI model proposes Earth as an internally forced toroidal electromagnetic structure. In toroidal systems:

Energy circulates in closed geometry.

Perturbations redistribute rather than accumulate.

Stability depends on symmetry preservation.

[!IMPORTANT]
Loss of toroidal symmetry implies the emergence of gradient concentration zones and nonlinear amplification.

2.2 Theoretical Foundations

The thermodynamic basis of open systems was developed by Ilya Prigogine, who demonstrated how dissipative structures emerge far from equilibrium.

Synergetic formalism from Hermann Haken describes how order parameters govern large-scale coherence.

Catastrophe geometry from René Thom provides mathematical language for abrupt transitions.
